In 2021, educational psychology professor [Barak Rosenshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Rosenshine) published his article _Principles of Instruction: Research-Based Strategies That All Teachers Should Know_, which quickly has become one of the most popular resources for teachers because it combines a solid research basis with actionable guidance about how to level up your instruction in the classroom.

/blog

These principles work best when applied consistently, and Examplary can help you extend them beyond classroom instruction to practicing outside of the classroom.

 
## Always begin with a short review of previous learning

Daily repetition and review helps solidify the connections between the things we've learned. 

_In the classroom_: 5-8 minutes of reviewing past knowledge and concepts with your students, letting students do peer homework review. For reviewing, focus on concepts that need to become automatic and will help students in further learning.

_Using Examplary_: Copy over a key question from a previous test, or let Examplary generate a variation on a previous question as the start in any practice test. You can also use **practice spaces** to focus specifically on review, by including topics from previous lessons, which Examplary's AI will intelligently mix in with questions about newer material.

## Present new material in small steps with student practice

Our working memory is limited, and learning works best if it's bite-sized.

_In the classroom_: Check students' understanding about each point of new material you're introducing before moving on to the next, and provide many concrete examples related to each concept.

_Using Examplary_: Create focussed practice spaces, spanning no more than the content from one lesson. Examplary automatically will sub-divide your content into small topics, to allow students to practice with each one and get insight into their understanding.

## Ask a large number of questions

Teachers who spend more time of their instruction asking questions, tend to be more successful in knowledge transfer.
